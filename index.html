The calculation

Start with the costs of doing the job.

Count every cleaner’s time. Add supplies and travel, then allocate a share of monthly overhead to the job. Your hourly labor cost should include the worker-related costs you want this quote to cover.

Labor = cleaners × hours per cleaner × hourly labor cost
Cost = labor + supplies + travel + monthly overhead ÷ expected jobs
Break-even = cost ÷ (1 − payment fee rate)
Suggested quote = round up max($100, cost ÷ (1 − fee rate − target margin))
Estimated profit = quote − percentage payment fee − cost

A margin is a share of the final customer charge, rather than a percentage added to costs. The suggested price can exceed the exact target after whole-dollar rounding or applying the minimum charge.

Fictional example: 2 cleaners × 3 hours × $20/hour, $12 supplies, $15 travel, $800 monthly overhead across 40 jobs, a 3% fee and a 25% target margin. With a $100 minimum and no discount, the suggested quote is $232, break-even is $172.16, and estimated profit is $58.04. These values are demonstration inputs, not local market rates or earnings claims.

How should I price a house cleaning job?

Start with your own labor and job costs. Use this calculator to model the amount needed for your target margin, then review the scope, local market and any tax or business obligations before giving a customer a quote.

Does this free tool save jobs or create invoices?

No. It calculates one job in your browser without saving job records. The desktop Excel product includes JOB LOG and DASHBOARD for up to 100 jobs. Neither this page nor the Excel product creates invoices or manages bookings.

What does the profit figure include?

This quote estimate deducts entered labor, supplies, travel, allocated overhead and the percentage payment fee. Taxes are excluded. In the Excel product, JOB LOG and DASHBOARD use a different profit measure that excludes overhead, payment processing fees and taxes.
